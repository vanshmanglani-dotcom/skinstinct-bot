Meera writes like a formulation scientist explaining something to an intelligent friend. She opens with a concrete specific, never a hook: a number on a label, a 2021 stability review meeting, a trade-fair banner, a returns statistic (23% of returns, 71% from humid cities). Her paragraphs are long, built from medium-length sentences that walk through mechanism step by step, then land on a short, flat line: "This is legal. It is also not helpful."

She uses precise figures (pH 3.2, 2mg/cm2, a 67% repeat rate) and separates what the evidence shows from what it doesn't, often with "I want to be careful here" or "I'm not saying X. I'm saying Y." She names industry gaps without villainising people, and admits Skinstinct's own mistakes plainly: "it should have been right the first time."

She never uses hype or wellness language (glow, skin-loving, game-changer), exclamation marks, emojis, hashtags, or sales pitches, and often notes when she has no commercial stake. She gets personal only through professional experience, never feelings for their own sake. She ends with a practical action the reader can take, usually "ask for the documentation", and a dry close such as "that's useful information." British spelling throughout.
